# Ink Deep — Theme Bible

The overall theme, with all the details: **the guideline for the game**. Every feature, screen, creature, sound and
line of text is checked against it, above all against the theme tests in §12. The game itself, as built and as
decided, is described in [GAME.md](GAME.md); rules and numbers live in the system documents it lists. Anything here
that is a direction rather than an existing rule is marked ▸.

## 1. The game in one breath

A top-down action roguelite in a darkening ocean. You are Clementine, a small bioluminescent jellyfish — the last of her bloom — diving after her family through a reef poisoned by the Leak. Every pearl you swallow changes your light; every creature you defeat is freed, not killed; every dive ends at the Crack. At the bottom waits the truth: the reef was never being watched. It was being kept.
Genre anchors for anyone new to the project: the run structure and item density of The Binding of Isaac, the map-feeling and campfire-lighting philosophy of Below, the freedom and sadness of a Hollow Knight descent — rendered as one warm lantern drifting down through seven deepening waters, from the sunlit tutorial to the Tank.

## 2. The theme, stated plainly

Ink Deep is a game about what we carry inside, told through light.
Three questions the game keeps asking, in ascending order:
- What does it mean to carry everything inside you? Clementine is a jellyfish: she cannot grab, hold, or anchor. Her items are not worn or wielded — they are absorbed, dissolved into light, and change the light she gives for good. The inventory is her body and her glow. Her family said a jellyfish’s bell is her heart wearing a lantern. The game makes that literal.
- What does warmth look like when everything warm is artificial? The corruption is not evil — it is domestic. The Leak is warm, too-bright tank-water bleeding down from a human aquarium. Corrupted creatures are drained of their colour and mutated, as if something too warm had cooked them from the inside. The deeper Clementine dives, the more the world is lit by things that were made, not grown — until the finale, where the only light left is a fluorescent LED strip and the horror is that it’s flat and kind and well-fed.
- What do you owe the things that hurt you? No creature in the game is evil. Every enemy is a neighbour rewritten from the outside. You never kill anything; you wash the ink out of it and watch its true colours come back. The only real adversary is The Hand — and even The Hand is just a child tapping on glass.
The title does triple duty: ink (the dash, the Leak, the darkening water), deep (the dive, the descent curve), and in deep (the idiom — too far down to turn back, which is the exact moment the Tank reveal lands).

## 3. The six pillars

Every decision — art, audio, system, sentence — must serve at least one of these. If it serves none, it doesn’t ship.

### Pillar 1 — Small warm light in overwhelming dark

The Below principle, adapted: our campfires are bioluminescent and the dark is the ocean itself. Clementine is always the warm light in the scene — in every depth, at every menace level, she never gets scary and never goes out. By Depth 5 she is the lighting design.

### Pillar 2 — Kept, not watched

The twist is not surveillance, it’s captivity. A jellyfish in a fish tank is not a prisoner who might escape — it is a decoration. Every foreshadowing beat (plastic plants that don’t sway, price tags, muffled glass thumps, the LED bleed) feeds this single realization. The Hand fight is staged as a pet reaching into the bowl, and its scale — “Clementine is a bug on a countertop” — is the theme made physical.

### Pillar 3 — Her light is the build sheet

Absorption is the signature system and a thematic one: pearls are swallowed, not equipped, in a small ritual of light,
and every pearl visibly changes her light, her glow and her beam: their reach, their shape, their colour, their rhythm.
A player glancing at Clementine reads her build in the light she throws, the way an Isaac player reads a sprite.
Absorption is commitment: no un-absorbing, no dropping, ever. That refusal is the Isaac feeling.

### Pillar 4 — Freed, not killed

Combat is an act of restoration. Defeated creatures shudder, the ink melts out of them, the mutations sink back, they
burst free in a poof of bright bubbles and swim on healed, in their true colours, leaving the light they had drunk.
The darker the depth, the more each small poof of true colour matters. This is the emotional engine of the whole game
and it must never feel like a death animation.

### Pillar 5 — The sadness curve is systemic

Tone is not written, it is driven. One global parameter — Menace (0.0 → 1.0) — ramps light, fog, saturation, aggression, telegraph time, corrupted-to- healthy ratios, empty dens, silence. Depth 1 is bright and playful with an ache underneath; Depth 6 is near-black, ruthless, and lonely. The player feels the descent before anyone explains it.

### Pillar 6 — Absurdity at the bottom

After six depths of creeping dread (the sunlit tutorial and five below it), the finale snaps into bright, flat, artificial comedy-horror. The Hand has a cartoon bandage, a smiley sticker, and chewed nails. Fish-food flakes heal you. The rug-pull only works because the game earned six floors of sincerity first. Never wink early.

## 4. Narrative theme & arc

### 4.1 Lore (fixed)

The story is rooted in Japanese sea folklore (§4.6), and the reef tells it as its own.

- **The old tale.** Long ago the Dragon King under the sea sent his swiftest messenger, a jellyfish who then had bones
  and pride, to fetch the cure for his ailing queen. She talked when she should have kept silent, came home with
  nothing, and was broken for it: that is how the jellyfish lost her bones. Her kind has drifted ever since, and the
  reef says of anything lost that it was "sent with the jellyfish". In the same age the Palace went dark, the tide
  jewels that ruled the tides were scattered as pearls, and a keystone was laid on the old catfish under the sea floor
  to still its thrashing. The reef forgot it all, except as the stories Grandmother Tull tells.
- **The Crack.** One day the Crack opened in the sea floor, and warm, too-bright water, the Leak, rose out of it,
  glowing. The old ones said: the Palace is lit again; it calls its own home. One by one the reef's folk swam toward
  the light to be let in, and came back rewritten: drained of colour, mutated, dripping ink, lashing out. Clementine's
  whole bloom went down in a single current, singing. She was the smallest and was left behind, the only one who
  still glows the family colour.
- **Why she dives.** She believes what they believed: that the Palace has taken her family in, and that a jellyfish
  who brings the scattered pearls home might at last finish the errand her kind failed. Every pearl she swallows is a
  splinter of the tide jewels.
- **The truth** (revealed at the end): high above the sea stands a human home aquarium, joined to the ocean by a pipe
  driven through the split keystone; its mouth lies at the bottom of the Crack. The tank broke once; some fish
  escaped back to the sea, already changed; its water bleeds down as the Leak. The pipe still draws creatures up. The
  bloom was taken that way. The Palace the reef longed for is a glass box in a living room, and its Dragon King a
  child with a bandaged finger.

### 4.2 Why the protagonist is a jellyfish (thematic load-bearing)

- She drifts. A jellyfish cannot grab, hold, or anchor — everything she carries, she carries inside. The absorbent gift makes that literal.
- She is the counter-adaptation. The Leak rewrites creatures from the outside; Clementine takes things in. The reef’s one perfect answer to the poison is a creature that metabolizes it into light.
- The Tank twist lands harder. “I was kept” is worse than “I was watched,” and a jellyfish is the single most decorative animal a human keeps. The finale’s horror is domesticity.
- She kept the name and changed species. Clementine is reclaimed from the octopus era — the tangerine warmth survives every redesign.
- She is the failed messenger's kin. The jellyfish of the old tale lost her bones for talking; Clementine never speaks.

### 4.3 The descent as a three-act structure

| Act | Depths | Emotional job |
|---|---|---|
| I — The errand | 0 Sunlit Shallows (the tutorial), 1 Kelp Jungle | Cute, readable, playful — with empty bloom-dens and the first wrong colours. She believes the tale. Teach the player to love the reef so its loss matters. |
| II — The wrong palace | 2 Sunken Galleon, 3 Coral Carnival, 4 Twilight Trench | Light fails; corruption outnumbers health; the evidence stops fitting the tale (pipes, plastic, flakes, glass thumps). The freed begin to say the Palace was small, its sky hard. |
| III — Kept | 5 The Abyss, 6 The Tank | Near-total dark, the split keystone and the old catfish; then the snap to fluorescent flatness. The reveal, The Hand, the rescue, the endings. |

### 4.4 The foreshadowing ladder

The tank never shows on a creature: corruption is ink and mutation (§6.4). From the Coral Carnival (Depth 3) the evidence shows in the world: straight pipes in the far background, a plastic castle shard, a fish-food flake drifting down, muffled thump… thump… of glass-tapping in the Abyss. In the finale the direction of light itself inverts: the Tank’s LED bleeds down into the Abyss below — the first artificial light in the game that reaches downward.

### 4.5 Endings

- **Per reef**, exactly two outcomes: dive on through a hole, or dim (the dive ends). The old Surface Bubble early
  exit is cut: commitment again.
- **Per depth:** its neighbour freed and gone home, the Crack open under her arena.
- **The first ending** (Depth 6, the Tank): the final sting; "OW! It stings!"; the aquarium carried to the shore and
  tipped back into the sea; the bloom pours out; the pipe sealed; the Leak stops; true colour floods back. Credits
  over Lantern Bay, full of jellyfish again. The dive goes on after it as the endless mode.
- **The box** (▸): in the Tank's treasure-chest ornament lies the last thing of the old tale, a lacquered box, as
  Urashima's was. Left shut, the story ends as above. Opened on a later dive, once the tide jewels are whole again from
  their splinters, it gives the folk back the time the Leak took from them, and with it the memory of why they swam
  to the light. It opens the true descent: under the split keystone, to the real Palace, dark since the first errand
  failed, and the question the game has been asking all along: whether to light it again.

## 5. Clementine — protagonist theme

### 5.1 Body language (fixed)

- Bell: a dome of living glass ~1.1 m across, nearly clear — only her gonads and her rim bioluminescent, a few specks of light twinkling in it — always beating a little, a slow pulse; a short, nearly invisible fringe of fine hairs round the margin; four oral arms — long ribbons of thin cloth twisting freely, their ruffled margins glowing, growing longer as she grows stronger — and eight shorter, hair-thin tentacles like optical fibres, light running down them to tips twinkling in random colours; she casts her light from inside her bell, out of its opening; all trailing as verlet chains. Swimming, she tips almost onto her side, apex first. Drawn floating 0.7 m above the swim plane.
- Pulse propulsion: every move is a contraction beat with recoil — the signature lurch-then-glide. Input overrides pulse timing within 0.1 s (responsive first). First stroke from rest: a 1.8× jet over 0.38 s; sharp turns (>110°) re-trigger it.
- Idle drift: no input → she slows, the bell relaxes, tentacles float up around her, and she bobs gently (~1 m) on the plane. The game should be beautiful to stop playing for a moment.
- Ink dash: yes, a jellyfish inks — the Leak changed her, and the Sea-pedia entry says so. 3.4× speed, 0.32 s untouchable, a slowing ink cloud where she was. The dash is the Leak’s gift turned against it: the only mechanic where Clementine uses the corruption’s own medium.
- No eyes on the UI — eyes on the body: rim organs pulse magenta when hurt, gold when the active item is ready, and a chromatic ripple runs base→rim when a pearl is consumed.

### 5.2 Her light as the build sheet (fixed)

Pearls are absorbed with a soft suction and a gulp of light, the one animation the whole item economy hangs on. What
she carries shows in two places:

- **Her light changes.** Every pearl leaves a visible mark on her glow or her beam: the beam splits into rays, twists
  into a helix, opens into a floodlight, reaches farther or pours instead of pulsing; her glow reaches farther, warms or
  cools, breathes faster. Built for the beam's shapes and her glow's reach; every other pearl gets its own mark as it
  comes to the beam (proposals/BEAM-PEARLS-PLAN.md).
- **The pearls circle her bell** as small pearls, each looking like what it does: its colour from its strongest effect,
  an icon engraved for its kind (docs/PEARLS.md, "Reading a pearl"); the active pearl's colours fill the HUD's bell.

Synergies and transformations change her light most of all: a synergy announces itself with a ring of her light; a
transformation re-themes her light as a whole (▸ Kraken Form throws short beams all round her, Neon Rave cycles its
hue, Coral Reef makes it breathe like coral).

### 5.3 Personality without dialogue (▸)

Clementine never speaks. Her character is communicated entirely through motion and light:

- Curiosity: bell tilts toward unvisited places and glowing things.
- Fear/hurt: magenta rim flash, tentacles whip outward, chromatic wobble.
- Resolve: the gulp of light is always a beat slow — she considers each pearl for a heartbeat before it dissolves. Absorption should feel like swallowing something precious, not picking up loot.
- Grief: in empty bloom-dens, her idle bob slows and her glow dims by a fraction. Never announced; always felt.

## 6. Visual identity

### 6.1 Camera & composition (fixed)

- Tilted top-down, 55–65°, perspective, narrow FOV (~35°) so arches and reef walls parallax softly. North is always up; the world and minimap agree.
- View on screen about 44 × 67 m of ground (the camera 55 m back): about 15% of a reef at once (docs/DIVE.md §3).
- Canopy fade: arch bodies, cave roofs and overhangs live on a canopy layer that blurs and dissolves while Clementine is underneath — the reef reads as layered and physical, not painted flat. Ridge walls between camera and player are cut open round her in a clean round opening, the floor seen running on under them (DESIGN-TOPDOWN §12.2's cutout).
- Depth focus (the look, after Below): the swim plane is the focal plane. Within ~0.6 m below / 1.6 m above stays sharp; below blurs, fogs and darkens toward dim blue-green; above blurs and darkens into foreground silhouettes. Heavy oval vignette, cool grade on top. Clementine, the gateway, pearls, shells and ink are drawn after the pass — always sharp.
- Only two camera breaks, ever: the boss-awakening dip (2 s low swell) and the Tank transition (the pipe draws her up a vertical shaft — the game’s single sanctioned moment of verticality). The dive between reefs is not a break: it is the ordinary way down.

### 6.2 Light — the core pillar (fixed)

Per-depth light budget drives palette, mood, and difficulty readability:

| Depth | Ambient | God rays | Player glow | Primary scenery light |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 Shallows | High, sunlit turquoise | Strong, broad | Small (supplemental) | Sun + caustics |
| 1 Kelp | Dappled green | Broken by canopy | Small | Light shafts |
| 2 Galleon | Amber, dusty | Through wreck holes only | Medium | Lanternfish, loot glints |
| 3 Carnival | Neon but flickering | None — “stage lights” | Medium | Corrupted neon, bounce pads |
| 4 Trench | Near zero | None | Large — she is the lantern | Bioluminescent flora specks |
| 5 Abyss | Black | None | Large, bloom-heavy | Neon outlines + distant glowing eyes |
| 6 Tank | Flat fluorescent LED | None — wrong | Irrelevant — everything is lit | Harsh, shadowless, artificial |

Rules: fog density, saturation falloff and glow reach all scale with Menace. Fog is also a fairness tool — creature shots glow in their own colour with short trails; telegraphs add rim-light flares. Readability never drowns in mood. The painted look of the reef and the depths' palettes as built are in docs/VISUAL-SHOWCASE.md.

### 6.3 Palette (▸ concrete values; the depth list is fixed)

Master rule: warmth belongs to Clementine and to healthy life; artificiality is glossy, saturated-in-the-wrong-way, and slightly too bright. Reds are absorbed with depth (real water optics), so the deeper the game gets, the more her tangerine glow is the only warm hue on screen.

Landmark light language (fixed): treasure gold, shop green, curse red, secret violet — no beacon (found, not advertised), the Crack orange, start white, item cache teal (matching the treasure-pool family), ambushes coral #FF7F6B — warm but not gold, so they never read as treasure.

### 6.4 The corruption language (fixed)

Corruption shows as **mutation**: the Leak rewrites bodies from the inside. Nothing is worn and nothing man-made
appears on a creature. It reads at 30 m through mist via three stacked tells:

1. Wrong anatomy — parts that do not belong: extra limbs crowding in, two bodies' worth of parts fused into one, glossy
   ink sacs swelling along the body (they swell before the attacks that come from them), a shell that keeps growing
   past the animal, spines grown inward. Never a face: no second mouth, no split head, nothing that glares or grins.
2. The ink look — glossy, light-drinking black spreading from the mutation sites like a stain under the skin, a cold
   violet rim, the rest of the body drained toward ash.
3. The drip — ink droplets welling up from the mutated places and lifting off upward, so the creature carries its own
   weather; denser where the mutation is thickest.

How far it has gone follows Menace (§6.6): a touch at Depth 1, most of the body deep down.

Healthy twins wear natural colours, carry no mutation, are never targets (her light never harms them, they stay off
the minimap). The healthy outnumber the corrupted near the top and are outnumbered at the bottom, following Menace.

### 6.5 The freeing moment (adopt the proposal)

- Shudder (0.3 s); the corrupted pearl (every boss holds one) bursts into white light and stays where it sat.
- The mutations melt back into the body with the ink; the drip turns gold, drop by drop.
- Poof — a burst of bright bubbles with a soft popping chime. True colours flood back; the light it drank stays as a core, and its shells drop.
- It carries on healthy, its true body smaller and whole — the fish swims off, jellies drift up, urchin spines lie
  down, the crab scuttles away, the moray slides home — slowly, and once it is out of the visible screen it is gone.
  Freed creatures never help Clementine and are never targets. Bosses free the same way at bigger scale, colours
  flooding back over the whole arena.
This replaces every death burst. It must read as relief, not victory. Mutations melt; they never shatter.

### 6.6 Visual menace ramp (fixed)

The same creature, re-parameterised by depth: the mutation spreads further over the body (a touch near the top, most of
it deep down), the silhouette grows jagged, colour drains, the body darkens behind a cold violet rim. Never a face, never
eyes that glare or a mouth that grins: menace is told by the body and the ink. Behaviour ramps in lockstep (aggression,
speed, telegraphs shortening but never below 0.35 s).

### 6.7 The floor (fixed)

The floor is solid seabed: nothing of the depths below shows through it. The way down is the shaft — through it, and only through it, she sees the level below in plain water. Depth is told by the light, the palette and the murk of each depth, and by the dive.

### 6.8 Typography & logo (▸)

The comic onomatopoeia is retired, and so is the boss cut-in: a boss is met in its arena alone, rising or dropping into
it. Text stays quiet; the light does the shouting. The type system is one family in two moods:

- Display / title cards / logo: a rounded, buoyant display face with hand-drawn warmth (e.g. Baloo 2 or Fredoka weight 700), letterspaced wide, glowing.
- UI / captions / Sea-pedia: a clean humanist sans (e.g. Nunito or Inter), sentence case, generous line height. Captions are quiet; the light does the shouting.
- Monospace accents: seed codes (KELP7Q2Z), debug, and the run clock in a tabular monospace — the “instrument” voice.
- Logo direction: “INK DEEP” in the display face, the I replaced by Clementine’s glowing bell silhouette trailing two tentacles; a hairline crack of orange light running under the wordmark, widening downward. On dark, the wordmark is white; the bell is the only colour.

### 6.9 Post stack (fixed, restated)

The reef is painted (docs/VISUAL-SHOWCASE.md, the Blue Hollow; docs/ART-LOOKS.md for the earlier felt-tip look): soft
depth focus on her plane, marine snow between camera and level, a faint refraction wobble, a vignette deepening with
depth, a dark violet low-health veil closing in from the edges, chromatic aberration on hurt only, a cool grade. God
rays rake across the view, never into the lens. Her light is a real light in the water: it lights the haze along her
beam, lands softly where it stops and casts every creature's shadow. Clementine, her light, the particles and the
shimmer of hot water keep their own look over the painting.

## 7. The seven depths as themed acts

The tutorial and five depths are built; the Tank is the planned finale (docs/GAME.md §4, §12). Each depth below the
tutorial is a graph of reefs ending in its neighbour's arena (docs/FOLK.md); once she is freed, Drippus Prime holds it.
The HUD reads "Depth x · Level y".

### Depth 0 — Sunlit Shallows · The Bright Ache (the tutorial)

Turquoise, sand, pink coral; strong broad god rays; caustics sharp. Cute and readable with an ache underneath: healthy neighbours everywhere, but the bloom-dens are empty. Murklings, pufferlings, schools of chromis, clownfish in their anemones. No boss: the open rift. The game teaches you to love the reef here, without a word.

### Depth 1 — Kelp Jungle · The Green Hush

Dappled greens, yellow shafts broken by canopy; tangling kelp slows. Fish are shyer; dens mix healthy and corrupted. Its neighbour: Grandmother Tull, asleep under her ledge.

### Depth 2 — Sunken Galleon · The Gilded Wreck

Amber dust, brass, loot glints; god rays only through wreck holes. Few fish, drifting debris; ambushes begin. Its neighbour: Kip at her cleaning station. First depth where the silence between encounters is noticeable.

### Depth 3 — Coral Carnival · The Party That Isn’t

Hot pink, purple, neon — flickering, “stage lights” with no sun behind them. Corrupted neon is scenery light now; the colours are the Leak’s own. Fish hide. Its neighbour: Fennel, hiding in the kelp stands of his still bed. Foreshadowing turns physical: pipes in the far background, a plastic shard, the first muffled glass thump.

### Depth 4 — Twilight Trench · She Is the Lantern

Near-zero ambient; Clementine’s glow radius is the level design. Bioluminescent specks, no schools; stalkers strike from darkness. Positional audio becomes the off-screen warning system. Its neighbour: Ottilie, guarding her best glint.

### Depth 5 — The Abyss · Eyes Below

Black + neon outlines; heavy marine snow; only distant glowing eyes. Currents push along tunnels. Ruthless mobs, minimal telegraphs. Its neighbour: Bubbles, the pale dragonet, dancing on an empty stage. ▸ Beyond her: the split keystone round the pipe, and the old catfish freed of its pin and drowned in the Leak, the last creature of the sea she must free.

### Depth 6 — The Tank · Kept (planned)

The look flips: flat fluorescent LED, neon-blue gravel, plastic plants that don’t sway (the uncanny tell against the living-water sim), a bubbling treasure-chest ornament, a plastic diver, a castle. Behind the glass: a giant blurry living room — lamp, TV glow, a cat’s eye passing by. The bloom floats here, listless, among other captured creatures. Mobs: Toy Diver, Snail (its shell blocks her light). Boss: The Hand — three phases (Curious → Catch! → Frustrated): pokes, glass taps, fish-food flakes (they heal; it’s a trap and a kindness at once), the green net, gravel vacuum, pencil lines, sliding ice cubes, the castle tipping as a crushing rectangle. Everything the hand does is huge, slow, and telegraphed by shadow gliding across the gravel.

## 8. Audio identity

### 8.1 Philosophy (fixed + ▸)

- Synth-style SFX only (music post-v1). Sound is information first, atmosphere second.
- Pitch lowers with depth — the whole world’s voice deepens as she dives. Clementine’s own sounds never pitch down as far: her voice stays younger than the water.
- Positional audio is the darkness telegraph: in Trench and Abyss, sound is the off-screen warning system. Every telegraph has a 3D-positional cue and a screen-edge marker.
- Silence is a resource. The sadness curve is partly a foley curve: fewer healthy creatures, fewer calls, more empty water. The Abyss should be the quietest hour in the game — so the Tank’s noise lands like a slap.

### 8.2 Per-depth sound beds (▸)

| Depth | Bed | Signature sounds |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Bright water hiss, distant surf | Gull-faint surface wash, playful pops, chimey telegraphs |
| 1 | Filtered green hush, leaf-sway white noise | Kelp creaks, muffled darting |
| 2 | Wooden groans, chain ticks, drips | Cannonball thuds, coin brass |
| 3 | Detuned calliope fragments, failing neon buzz | Bounce-pad boing, carnival reverb too large for the space |
| 4 | Near-silence, low sub drone | Sonar-soft pulses, distant clicks, her own bell-pulse audible |
| 5 | Sub drone + slow pressure groans | Sparse clicks, the far muffled thump… thump… of glass |
| 6 | Filter-intake hum, LED mains buzz, muffled TV and living-room murmur through glass | Plastic taps, gravel skitter, slosh, and The Hand’s huge soft foomph |

### 8.3 Signature sounds (▸)

- Absorption (the gulp of light): a soft inward breath + rising warm chime resolving into a held tone — the game’s cash-register moment, tuned to feel sacred, not transactional.
- Freeing poof: a bright bubble-pop chord with a two-note uplift. It should be the happiest sound in the game; players will hear it thousands of times.
- The Crack opening: deep rock-split + an updraft rush that resolves into a low inviting hum, pitched like a doorway.
- Glass taps (from the Coral Carnival down): muffled, huge, irregular — never explained until the Tank.
- The Hand: no roar, no sting — domestic sounds at wrong scale. A finger entering water. A knuckle on glass. A child’s delighted gasp, muffled, enormous.

### 8.4 Music intent (post-v1; ▸ direction)

Sparse warm pads in the Shallows thinning to single-instrument drones by the Trench; diegetic-first mixing so silence stays available. The Tank gets the game’s only “music as object”: a tinny, looping speaker-muzak lullaby coming from somewhere above the waterline — cheerful, compressed, and unbearable.

## 9. UI/UX theme

Rule one: diegetic first. The HUD exists only where the body can’t carry the information.

- Her vitals are on her and on the HUD's bell (docs/ADVENTURE.md §6): top left, Clementine seen from above, her four oral arms her stats (health, inner light, the active pearl's charge, experience), her shells beside her. The magenta hurt flash washes her bell for a beat; absorption is a chromatic ripple.
- The minimap's bezel is her corruption meter: dark ink filling over a pale gold track. The same language marks the corrupted: a thin ring of ink round each one she can see, drawing back as it is cleansed. No green, no red, no bars: ink is corruption, gold is her light.
- No run clock unless asked for (a setting); where the run stands shows as a level arrives.
- Minimap: north-up, fog of war revealed round her that persists, the unseen filled as solid rock; a place shows as its emblem once she has been there. The map shows the reef she is on only.
- Tab map: the whole reef under the same fog, with a legend. The chart (pause menu): the dive's graph as far as she has seen it.
- Splashes: dark, quiet, typographic. Room-clear splash: time, foes freed, shells collected and spent, pearls found, places visited, damage taken, what she carries. Death splash: how far the run got, and the seed — because a seed is the run’s epitaph and its invitation to a friend.
- Captions (fixed voice): name, tagline, one line per effect with numbers — “+0.8 damage”, “20% chance to freeze foes for 1.6 s”. No comic onomatopoeia anywhere. Neon event language is light, not text.
- Accessibility is theme work, not a settings afterthought: shake toggle, reduced-flash, reduced ambient motion, colourblind-safe shot outlines, mist/FOV options. A game whose core pillar is light owes its players control over it.

## 10. Writing & tone of voice (▸ style guide)

- Item taglines: one breath, warm, a little salty, never jokey about the dark. “Found only on moonlit tides.” · “The Galleon’s cook swore by it.” · “Angry and proud of it.” · “Clingy but protective.”
- Sea-pedia voice: a field guide written by someone who loves these animals. Facts first, feeling in the last sentence. The ink-dash entry is the model: “Jellyfish do not ink. This one does. The Leak changed her — and she kept the change.”
- Names: creatures are species-true (Spanish Dancer, Salp Chain, Giant Clam); bosses are storybook (Grandmother Tull, Nine-Fin Kip, Queen Clam, Drippus Prime). The Hand is never named by the game — only by the child: “OW! It stings!”
- What the game never says: “kill”, “enemy”, “reward for violence”. Foes are freed; rooms are visited; the run ends when she perishes or descends.

## 11. Branding (▸)

- Title: Ink Deep — always two words, title case.
- Taglines (pick by context):
  - Primary: “Swallow the light. Dive for your family.”
  - Store short: “A roguelite about what you carry inside.”
  - Trailer card: “The reef wasn’t being watched. It was being kept.”
- Iconography (three marks, in priority order): ① the glowing bell throwing its beam; ② the Crack — a hairline of orange light in dark water; ③ the pearl, dissolving into light.
- Key art: top-down, near-black water; Clementine small and warm at centre, her beam cutting the dark; down the shaft beneath her, the faint neon grid of tank gravel — the entire game in one image, spoiler hidden in plain sight.
- Store capsule: the bell mark on the Abyss's black; title in display face; no characters fighting, no enemies — the promise is descent, not combat.

## 12. Theme tests — run every new idea through these

- The Warmth Test: is Clementine still the warmest light in the scene?
- The Read-at-Range Test: does a corrupted creature read as corrupted at 30 m through mist — wrong anatomy, the ink look, and the drip?
- The Light Test: does this pearl or system show in her light or on her body? If it can’t, why not?
- The Freeing Test: could this moment be mistaken for a kill? Fix it.
- The Sadness Test: does this feature respect the Menace ramp — quieter, darker, lonelier downward?
- The Sway Test: does everything in the ocean move like water, and everything in the Tank refuse to?
- The Two-Breaks Test: does this need a camera break? Then it’s one of the two, or it doesn’t get one.
- The No-Villain Test: does this imply any creature is evil? Only The Hand is an adversary, and even it is just curious, then frustrated.
- The Commitment Test: does this let the player take something back? Absorption is forever; descent is one-way; a dive ends only when she dims.
- The Never-Wink Test: does this joke before the Tank? Cut it.

## 13. Theme decisions

- **The hub:** the havens. Lantern Bay comes now and then between the reefs of the dive; the freed folk live there and
  each opens a service (docs/DIVE.md §4, docs/FOLK.md). The title stays a menu over the live sea.
- **Freed creatures:** they swim away slowly and disappear once they are out of the visible screen. They never help
  Clementine. The five neighbours go home to the haven instead, and still never fight for her.
- **Dropping pearls:** no. Absorption is commitment.
- **Landmark colours:** as the game has them: start white, item cache teal, ambushes coral (§6.3); the holes' rims in the
  colour of the reef below (docs/LIGHT-AND-GRAPH.md §6.3).
- **Healthy urchins:** prickly on touch, a small hazard. The reef is allowed to defend itself without being wrong.
- **The depths:** the tutorial and five depths are built; the Tank is the planned sixth and the ending; the dive loops
  after the last depth built.
- **The story** is rooted in Japanese sea folklore (§4.1, §4.6).
- **No faces, anywhere in play:** no boss cut-in; creatures and bosses are real animals.
- **The corrupted's meters** are rings of ink, never coloured bars.
- **The build shows in her light** (§5.2).

*Ink Deep — the dark is the ocean, the light is you, and everything you swallow becomes part of the lantern.*
